Ecological & Physical Rationale

1. Temperature & Humidity (>50% Combined)

Regulate vapor pressure deficit. When temperature exceeds 30°C and humidity drops below 35%, fine dead fuels desiccate into highly flammable kindling.

2. Wind Speed

Bends flames forward to preheat unburnt fuel ahead and lofts burning embers over long distances.

3. NDVI & Terrain Slope

Low NDVI indicates senescent dry biomass. Slopes over 15° create an upward chimney effect, doubling the rate of spread.

Problem Statement

Forest fires cause severe ecological, environmental, and economic damage. Existing prediction methods often fail to accurately identify fire-prone regions because of the complex relationships among weather, vegetation, terrain, and human activities. This project develops a machine learning-based forest fire risk prediction system that analyzes historical and real-time environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, vegetation, and terrain data to predict high-risk areas and support early warning and disaster management.

Project Objectives

  • 1. To develop a machine learning model to predict forest fire risk using historical fire and environmental data.
  • 2. To compare Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine models to identify the best-performing algorithm.
  • 3. To generate forest fire risk maps to support early warning, resource allocation, and disaster prevention.
