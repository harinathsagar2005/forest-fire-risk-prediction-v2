Physics & Ecological Interpretability

1. Temperature & Relative Humidity (Primary Drivers)

Drive fuel moisture depletion. As temperatures exceed 30°C and RH falls below 35%, ambient vapor pressure deficit causes fine dead fuels (grasses, leaf litter) to reach flashpoint rapidly.

2. Wind Speed (Fire Spread Multiplier)

Winds above 20 km/h bend flames forward, preheating unburnt fuels via convective heat transfer and generating spotting ember showers.

3. NDVI & Topographic Slope

Low NDVI indicates senescent or cured dry biomass. Steeper slopes produce an upward chimney effect, accelerating wildfire spread rate up to 4x compared to flat terrain.

Problem Statement

Forest fires cause severe ecological, environmental, and economic damage. Existing prediction methods often fail to accurately identify fire-prone regions because of the complex relationships among weather, vegetation, terrain, and human activities. This project develops a machine learning-based forest fire risk prediction system that analyzes historical and real-time environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, vegetation, and terrain data to predict high-risk areas and support early warning and disaster management.

Project Objectives

  • Objective 1: To develop a machine learning model to predict forest fire risk using historical fire and environmental data.
  • Objective 2: To compare Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine models to identify the best-performing algorithm.
  • Objective 3: To generate forest fire risk maps to support early warning, resource allocation, and disaster prevention.
