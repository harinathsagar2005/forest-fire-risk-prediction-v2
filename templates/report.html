4. Problem Statement

Forest fires cause severe ecological, environmental, and economic damage. Existing prediction methods often fail to accurately identify fire-prone regions because of the complex relationships among weather, vegetation, terrain, and human activities. This project develops a machine learning-based forest fire risk prediction system that analyzes historical and real-time environmental factors such as temperature, humidity, rainfall, wind speed, vegetation, and terrain data to predict high-risk areas and support early warning and disaster management. Multiple machine learning algorithms are trained and evaluated to identify the most accurate prediction model.

5. Project Objectives

  • • Objective 1: To develop a machine learning model to predict forest fire risk using historical fire and environmental data.
  • • Objective 2: To compare Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine models to identify the best-performing algorithm.
  • • Objective 3: To generate forest fire risk maps to support early warning, resource allocation, and disaster prevention.

6. Abstract

Forest fires cause significant damage to ecosystems, biodiversity, and human life, making early prediction essential. This project presents a machine learning-based framework for predicting forest fire risk using environmental and geographical data. The system analyzes historical fire records along with meteorological parameters such as temperature, humidity, precipitation, and atmospheric pressure. It also considers vegetation and terrain features including NDVI, elevation, slope, and distance to roads. Data preprocessing techniques such as data cleaning, normalization, and feature selection are applied to improve prediction accuracy and enhance model performance. Three machine learning algorithms—Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine (SVM) are implemented and compared to identify the most effective prediction model. Among these, Random Forest achieves the highest prediction accuracy (95.0%) and effectively identifies forest fire risk levels. The proposed model classifies forest regions into low, medium-, and high-risk categories based on the analyzed environmental conditions. The framework is designed to be scalable and can be adapted to different forest regions by incorporating region-specific environmental and geographical data, making it suitable for real-world applications. The prediction results support forest authorities in early warning, efficient resource allocation, wildfire prevention, and emergency response planning. By providing accurate and timely fire-risk assessments, the proposed system helps reduce environmental and economic losses while promoting sustainable forest management, ecological conservation, and long-term disaster preparedness.

Keywords: Forest Fire, Machine Learning, Random Forest, SVM, Logistic Regression, NDVI, SHAP

7. Experimental Outcomes & Performance Evaluation

The models were trained on 3,000 multi-spectral environmental and topographical records using an 80:20 stratified train-test split. The evaluation metrics demonstrate that the non-linear ensemble nature of Random Forest significantly outperforms both linear hyperplanes (Logistic Regression) and kernel projections (SVM).

8. Explainable AI (XAI) & SHAP Analysis

To eliminate the "black-box" dilemma in mission-critical disaster management, SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) using TreeExplainer was integrated. The results indicate that:

  • Temperature and Relative Humidity accounted for over 54% of total predictive importance, directly validating fire meteorology physics where vapor pressure deficits govern fine fuel desiccation.
  • Wind Speed functions as an active multiplier, accelerating rate of spread and ember transport.
  • NDVI and Slope provide structural terrain influence, showing that slopes > 18° accelerate fire spread uphill due to convective pre-heating.

9. GIS Risk Mapping & Early Warning System Deliverables

Pursuant to Objective 3, an interactive GIS Mapping dashboard was deployed using Leaflet.js with multi-layer geospatial feeds:

High Risk (Tier 1)

Siren alarm activation, pre-positioning water tankers, drone thermal monitoring, and tourist trail closures.

Medium Risk (Tier 2)

Elevated lookout tower observation, fire break inspections, and community SMS advisories.

Low Risk (Tier 3)

Routine telemetry health pings and standard conservation operating routines.

10. Generative AI: LLM Wildfire Incident Copilot & Bilingual Advisory

To bridge the operational gap between raw ML predictions and on-the-ground disaster intervention, an advanced Large Language Model (LLM) Incident Copilot module was implemented. Leveraging dual-mode execution (Google Gemini 1.5 Flash API + built-in zero-latency domain knowledge engine), the module automatically transforms multi-sensor telemetry into:

  • Executive Disaster Briefings: Formal situational summaries detailing fuel moisture depletion, convective flame vectors, and terrain chimney acceleration for the District Disaster Management Authority (DDMA).
  • Tactical Asset Deployment Plans: Automated calculation of required rapid intervention water tenders, FLIR thermal surveillance drones, and mechanical firebreak clearance crews.
  • Bilingual Emergency Broadcasts: Formatted emergency text alerts generated in both English and Telugu (for rural communities and panchayats bordering the Amrabad / Nallamala forest reserves).
  • Interactive Officer Chatbot: A real-time conversational agent capable of addressing viva inquiries, explaining feature contributions, and providing step-by-step Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
